# Reviewed, bounded torrent seeding

Downloads stop on completion by default. On a completed, fully paused native torrent, open **Seeding policy**, choose 1–1440 minutes and optionally a lifetime upload-ratio goal of 0.01–100 (0 for time only), approve sharing its files with peers, then start. Peers receive the torrent's downloaded content and can see the user's network address; existing tracker/privacy restrictions and the shared upload cap remain in force. This also works with private torrents using their existing trackers, without enabling public discovery or changing tracker membership.

Changing duration or ratio, refreshing, switching transfers or stopping clears approval. Stale responses from a previous selection cannot replace the current policy view. Active uploads appear as **Seeding**, with upload speed, and can be paused or stopped. **Stop sharing and clear policy** cancels the saved sharing choice. Removing a transfer also closes its peers and retains its downloaded files.

The native database saves the absolute UTC deadline, optional integer-percent ratio and intended started state in the same transaction before sharing starts. The ratio is lifetime uploaded payload divided by the torrent content size excluding padding. The target is rounded up to whole bytes with checked arithmetic; an already reached goal is refused. Sharing stops when either the time or ratio goal is reached. The status view labels upload accounting as the last policy check; Refresh seeding status fetches a fresh snapshot. A restart may resume an approved session only within its original deadline; it never extends the approved duration. An expired session stays stopped even if the process died while sharing. An ordinary Resume after expiry or a reached ratio retains stop-on-complete behavior. A restart never automatically shares when its persisted upload count has reached the saved ratio target. Expiry is checked by the run-loop's one-second ticker and stops peers even if the state write fails. These times depend on the operating-system clock; OS suspension/process scheduling and tracker stop messages are not exact real-time guarantees.

An unsuccessful start/save leaves the previous choice active. Stop always closes runtime sharing even if saving fails, and reports that the previous saved deadline may resume after a later restart. This distinction is necessary when durable storage is unavailable; the app does not claim that an unsuccessful disk write persisted revocation. Original deadline records are retained on invalid-state recovery rather than silently replaced.

The ratio is a best-effort accounting target, not an exact data quota. Payloads already in flight, the one-second policy check and upload counters lost since the last crash checkpoint can cause overshoot. Protocol traffic is excluded; uploaded padding blocks can be counted as payload. The existing finite time limit and upload-rate cap still apply independently. A hard per-torrent traffic budget would require separate durable reservation accounting and remains open.

Owner-only `GET /api/torrent-seeding?gid=…` returns `{until,ready,active,ratioPercent,targetBytes,uploadedBytes}`. A POST requires `{gid,minutes,approved}` and optionally `ratioPercent` (integer 0–10000; 100 means ratio 1.0); positive minutes require explicit approval and a complete paused torrent. Zero minutes stops without new approval and requires a zero/omitted ratio. Omitting ratioPercent retains the original time-only contract. Input is bounded to 1 KiB and protected by existing bearer/Host/Origin checks. Browser pairing tokens and WASI add-ons gain no sharing permission.

Acceptance uses original loopback fixtures: real peer upload with exact byte comparison, runtime deadline shutdown, future-deadline restart without extension, expired crash-state refusal, no expired tracker announcement, failed-save preservation and stop behavior, owner/Origin/consent refusal, and real browser controls. `scripts/seeding_integration.py` additionally kills an actual sharing app process, waits through its one-minute deadline and restarts it, verifying no sharing or repeated source request and unchanged downloaded bytes. Hosted results remain separate from local test evidence. Ratio acceptance additionally covers a real peer reaching its goal before the time limit, exact received bytes, future-goal restoration without extension, a modeled crash with Started=true/already reached accounting and no tracker contact, explicit ordinary Resume refusal, reviewed higher goals, failed-save preservation/stop, malformed saved policies and overflow boundaries. Actual in-app browser tests cover ratio edit/refresh approval revocation, active policy display and explicit stop. Hard per-torrent upload budgets and broader public/private swarm acceptance remain open.
